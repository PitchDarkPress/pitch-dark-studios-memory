THE FACTUAL LAYER — WHAT THE CARDS NEED BEFORE ANYTHING CAN BE CHECKED

WHY THIS EXISTS. The cards as built are for THINKING — Personality,
Motivation, Secrets, Arc, Backstory, long prose fields. Good for that, and
nearly useless for checking. "A tall man, greying now, with the sort of
face that gives nothing away" reads as writing, not as facts, and nothing
can be checked against it.
The continuity check needs facts. Barnaby has dark hair or he does not.

THE TEST FOR EVERY FIELD BELOW: A WRONG ANSWER MUST BE CHECKABLE.
Hair colour passes. Personality does not. If the platform cannot say
"this disagrees with what you wrote before" and be right or wrong about
it, the field belongs in the prose half of the card, not here.

A SECOND TEST, WORTH APPLYING AS THE LIST IS BUILT: does anyone actually
get this wrong? A field nobody ever contradicts is clutter. The ones
flagged *** below are the ones that catch writers most often.

HOW IT WORKS ONCE BUILT. The book is read ONCE, and what it finds lands
in these fields. From then on the CHECK RUNS AGAINST THE CARDS, not
against the book. You write blond in book two; Barnaby's card says dark;
the platform pings. The book goes quiet on the shelf.

================================================================
CHARACTERS
================================================================
*** NAME — the canonical spelling. So a lost or gained letter between
    books is caught. The commonest error of all and the most embarrassing.
    (Aliases already exist on the card as ALSO CALLED.)
*** HAIR COLOUR
*** EYE COLOUR
    HAIR — length and style, at the story's start
    HEIGHT — a figure or a plain word (tall, short, average)
    BUILD
*** AGE at the story's start
    DATE OF BIRTH, where the book fixes one
    HANDEDNESS
    SCARS, MARKS, TATTOOS, BIRTHMARKS
    GLASSES, HEARING AID, WALKING STICK, PROSTHETIC — anything carried on
    the body that a scene would have to acknowledge
    LIMP, TREMOR OR OTHER STANDING PHYSICAL TRAIT
    ACCENT
    VOICE — pitch, roughness, anything a reader would hear twice
    OCCUPATION at the story's start
    WHERE THEY LIVE — links to a Location
    LIVING OR DEAD, and if dead, when. A character who dies in book one
    and speaks in book two is a real and common slip in a series.
    SMOKER — yes or no
    DRINKER — and what they drink
    LEFT- OR RIGHT-SIDE HABITS, where the book has fixed one (which
    shoulder they carry a bag on, which hand holds the pistol)

================================================================
RELATIONSHIPS
================================================================
*** EXACT RELATION — cousin, half-brother, stepfather. "Family" is not
    enough; cousins becoming siblings between books happens.
    WHO IS OLDER
    WHEN THEY MET — a year, or the event
    WHETHER EITHER KNOWS A GIVEN SECRET, and from when. This is the one
    that produces the "nobody could have known that yet" fault.

================================================================
FACTIONS & ORGS
================================================================
    FOUNDED — when
    HEADQUARTERS — links to a Location or Building
    CURRENT LEADER — links to a Character
    SIZE — rough number
    COLOURS, EMBLEM, UNIFORM
    STILL EXISTS, OR DISBANDED, AND WHEN

================================================================
LANGUAGE & DIALOGUE
================================================================
    CATCHPHRASES — already on the card, and genuinely checkable
    WORDS THIS CHARACTER NEVER USES — a swearer who suddenly does not, or
    a character who never says a name and then does
    FORM OF ADDRESS — what they call each of the main characters. Sir,
    Father, Bill, Billy. A character who has always said "Father" and
    switches to "Dad" is either a moment or a mistake, and the platform
    should ask which.

================================================================
LOCATIONS
================================================================
*** COUNTRY OR COUNTY
    TYPE — village, town, city, island, region
    POPULATION — rough
    CLIMATE
*** DISTANCE AND TRAVEL TIME TO THE OTHER MAIN LOCATIONS. Matters more
    than it looks. A journey that takes an afternoon in book one and two
    days in book two is a common slip, and neither figure looks wrong on
    its own.
    DIRECTION from the other main locations — north of, downriver from
    ON THE COAST, INLAND, ON A RIVER
    STILL STANDING, OR DESTROYED, AND WHEN

================================================================
BUILDINGS
================================================================
    LOCATION — links to a Location; already on the card
*** NUMBER OF STOREYS
    BUILT OF — stone, brick, timber
    BUILT WHEN
*** OWNER at the story's start — links to a Character
    CELLAR — yes or no. Cellars get invented when a scene needs one.
    ATTIC — the same
    NUMBER OF ROOMS OF NOTE, and what they are
    ENTRANCES — how many ways in, and where
    STAIRCASE — where it is and where it comes out
    STILL STANDING, OR DESTROYED, AND WHEN

================================================================
OBJECTS & ARTEFACTS
================================================================
    MADE OF
    COLOUR
    SIZE — and weight where it matters to a scene
    MAKER OR MAKER'S MARK
*** WHO HAS IT NOW — links to a Character. The one that catches people
    out, because objects change hands and nobody tracks it.
    WHERE IT IS KEPT
    CONDITION — new, worn, broken, mended
    DISTINGUISHING MARKS — an inscription, a chip, an initial
    DESTROYED OR LOST, AND WHEN

================================================================
RULES & LORE
================================================================
Hard rules are checkable even when the world is invented. If magic costs
blood in book one, someone casting freely in book two is a fault.
    WHAT IT CAN DO
    WHAT IT CANNOT DO — the limits, stated plainly
    WHAT IT COSTS
    WHO CAN USE IT, AND WHO CANNOT
    HOW IT IS LEARNED
    TECHNOLOGY LEVEL — what exists in this world and what does not

================================================================
EVENTS & TIMELINE
================================================================
*** WHEN IT HAPPENED — a date, or an offset from another event
    HOW LONG IT LASTED
    WHERE — links to a Location or Building
    WHO WAS PRESENT — links to Characters
    WHO KNOWS ABOUT IT AFTERWARDS, AND FROM WHEN

================================================================
ACROSS THE WHOLE PROJECT
================================================================
*** THE YEAR THE STORY IS SET IN
*** HOW MUCH TIME PASSES from the first page to the last
    THE SEASON AT THE START
    CURRENCY IN USE
    UNITS — imperial or metric, and stay with one
    NAMING CONVENTIONS — whether titles are used, how names are spelled
    across the series

================================================================
THE NOTES FIELD ON EVERY RECORD
================================================================
Kev's addition, and it is the right one. Not every checkable fact fits a
named field, and the ones that do not are often the most characterful —
preferences, habits, the small fixed things a reader remembers.
"Drinks only tea." "Cannot abide dogs." "Always sits facing the door."
"Never removes his gloves." Each of those is CHECKABLE: a character who
orders coffee in book two is a fault, and it is exactly the kind of fault
that gets through.

SO EVERY RECORD CARRIES A NOTES FIELD FOR FACTS THAT DO NOT FIT A FIELD.

*** ONE THING TO SETTLE BEFORE BUILDING IT, AND IT IS A REAL QUESTION ***
THE CARDS ALREADY HAVE A NOTES FIELD. Characters, Locations, Buildings,
Objects and the rest each carry "notes" today, and it is a free prose box
for anything at all.
So there are two choices and they lead to different builds:
  1. USE THE EXISTING NOTES FIELD and have the checker read it. Simplest,
     nothing new on the screen. But it is full of prose, and a checker
     reading prose is back to guessing.
  2. A SECOND, SEPARATE LIST — one short line per fact, added one at a
     time, sitting with the other factual fields rather than with the
     prose. "Drinks only tea" as its own line, not buried in a paragraph.
     More to build, far more checkable.
CLAUDE'S VIEW: the second. A list of short lines is checkable; a
paragraph is not, and the whole point of this layer is that it is
checkable. But it does mean two Notes boxes on one card, which needs the
naming to be clear — the prose one is for thinking, the list is for facts.
NOT RULED ON BY KEV.

================================================================
STILL OPEN
================================================================
· WHICH WINS WHEN THE FACTS AND THE PROSE DISAGREE — the field says grey,
  the description says dark. Claude's instinct: the facts win for
  checking, and the tool FLAGS THE DISAGREEMENT rather than silently
  choosing. Not ruled on. Carried from 31 August.
· HOW MANY OF THESE FIELDS APPEAR AT ONCE. The full list above on one
  card is a form nobody would fill in. Likely they are folded away behind
  something, or only the *** ones show until asked for. NOT DESIGNED.
· WHETHER A FIELD LEFT EMPTY IS SILENT OR ASKS. A writer who has not said
  what colour Barnaby's eyes are should not be nagged — but the platform
  could offer to fill it from the book. Behind the muster, as always.
· THE FACTS BUILD THE DESCRIPTION. Carried from 31 August and still
  right: the card assembles a plain sentence from the fields and offers
  it; the writer writes over it in their own words; the facts stay
  underneath regardless. Nothing entered twice.
